# Question lenses by subject type

Pick the lens that matches what the user brought. They tell you which branches the design tree almost always has, so you don't forget a whole limb. They are starting points, not checklists — the tree the user actually has is what you grill, and many sessions mix lenses (an idea becomes a plan halfway through).

The root question for every lens is the same: **what does success look like, and for whom?** If that is not settled, nothing below it can be.

---

## Requirements (要件)

Something will be built or bought. The user usually starts from features; pull them back to purpose.

Branches that are almost always there:

- **Users and actors** — who uses it, who operates it, who approves it, who is affected but never touches it. Different actors have different requirements; "the user" is nearly always two or three people.
- **Job to be done** — what each actor is trying to accomplish, in their words. Features come after.
- **Scope and non-goals** — what is explicitly out. The non-goals are as valuable as the goals; they stop scope creep before it starts.
- **Acceptance** — how will anyone know it works? Concrete conditions, numbers where possible.
- **Constraints** — existing systems it must fit, data it must respect, budget, deadline, compliance, vendor lock-in.
- **Edge cases and failure** — what happens when the input is wrong, the network is down, two people do the same thing at once, someone cancels halfway. Invent scenarios.
- **Non-functional** — volume, latency, availability, security, language/locale. Usually unstated; usually decisive.
- **Ownership after launch** — who maintains it, who answers when it breaks.

## Business / product plan (企画)

Something will be offered and, usually, sold. The user often starts from the offering; pull them to the customer and the money.

- **Customer and problem** — who has the pain, how do they solve it today, what does the current workaround cost them.
- **Value proposition** — why this beats the workaround. Be suspicious of "better/cheaper/easier" without a number.
- **Why now** — what changed that makes this possible or urgent. If nothing changed, why hasn't it been done?
- **Revenue and pricing** — who pays, how much, how often, and who in the buyer's organization signs.
- **Unit economics** — cost to serve one customer vs. what they pay. Rough is fine; "unknown" is a finding.
- **Competition and alternatives** — including "do nothing" and the internal team that could build it.
- **Distribution** — how customers find out. The best plans die here.
- **Smallest test** — what is the cheapest experiment that would prove or kill the core assumption?
- **Risks and kill criteria** — what result would make the user stop?

## Project plan (計画)

Work will be organized and executed. The user often has a task list; pull them to the goal, the dependencies, and the people.

- **Goal and definition of done** — one sentence, and the date it must be true by.
- **Milestones** — the 3–6 points where something is demonstrably true, not a list of tasks.
- **Dependencies** — what must exist before each milestone; who outside the team it depends on; what they've actually committed to.
- **People and capacity** — who does the work, how much of their time is really available, what else they're on.
- **Decision rights** — who can change scope, who approves the release, who gets to say no.
- **Risks** — the two or three things most likely to blow the date, and what happens if they do.
- **Communication** — who needs to hear what, how often. Especially across language or company boundaries.
- **What gets cut first** — if the date is fixed and something must give, what?

## Raw idea (アイデア)

Nothing is decided yet. Don't force it into a plan too early; first find the core insight and whether it deserves a plan.

- **The one-line insight** — what does the user believe that others don't (or don't act on)?
- **Who cares** — name a specific person or role who would want this tomorrow. If the user can't, that's the first finding.
- **What it displaces** — what does that person stop doing?
- **Why it hasn't happened** — genuine obstacle, or nobody tried, or it has and failed (look that up).
- **What form it takes** — product, service, internal tool, content, side business. Different forms lead to different plans.
- **Smallest test** — one conversation, one landing page, one prototype, one weekend. What would tell them something real?
- **What the user is actually optimizing for** — money, learning, credibility, fun. This changes every downstream recommendation.

## Proposal / pitch to someone else (提案)

The user needs to convince a specific person or committee. Grill the audience, not just the content.

- **Audience** — who decides, what they care about, what they've said yes and no to before.
- **The ask** — exactly what the user wants them to approve: money, headcount, time, permission.
- **Their objections** — the two or three things they will say; the user's honest answers.
- **Evidence** — what the user has, what they're asserting, what they'd need to gather.
- **Alternatives the audience will think of** — including doing nothing.
- **What happens after yes** — the first 30 days, so the decision feels safe.

## Cross-lens tactics

- **Scenario generator**: take any rule or relationship the user states and construct the awkward case — the cancellation, the refund, the second user, the holiday, the customer who does it wrong. Ask what happens.
- **Term audit**: whenever the user uses a noun that could mean two things (user, customer, order, launch, done, team, plan), pin it. These become the glossary.
- **One-level-up**: when the user states a preference ("we should use X"), ask what it buys them. The constraint one level up often changes the answer.
- **Who-would-know**: when the user can't answer, ask who can. Record the question for that person instead of stalling.
